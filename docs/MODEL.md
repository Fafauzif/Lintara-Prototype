# Reproducible station model

`src/simulation/engine.ts` exports `simulate(scenario)`, `compareScenarios(scenario,start,end)`, and `createStressScenario(scenario)`. The engine imports station configuration and domain contracts. It imports no React, WebGL, or Three.js code. Rendering quality, camera, floor visibility, and frame rate cannot alter analytical outcomes.

## Authored horizon and demand

The scenario clock runs from 07:00 to 08:00 Asia/Jakarta: 0–3,600 simulated seconds. All report and intervention windows are half-open `[start,end)`. A snapshot at `t` represents the instant just before `t`; its people stock therefore equals arrivals before `t` minus departures before `t`, exactly matching `metrics(0,t)`. Stock in a period report is the station stock at its end, not that period's arrival count.

Demand is an authored planning assumption, not observed KRL passenger data. New plans default to `crowd:'normal'`; the optional field accepts only `normal` or `busy`. Version-1 imported plans without the field keep the original demand configuration instead of silently adopting the new default.

| Assumption | Authored multiplier | Background candidate slots over one hour | Scheduled train alighters |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Legacy: `crowd` omitted | 1 | 1,200 | Seeded 65–85 per train |
| Normal | 1.6 | 1,920 | Legacy seeded count × 1.6, rounded |
| Busy | 2.8 | 3,360 | Legacy seeded count × 2.8, rounded |

Every background candidate retains keyed 0.65 inclusion; the original three-second slot spacing and sub-slot jitter are divided by the multiplier. Actual sampled background totals therefore approximate the multiplier rather than guaranteeing an exact ratio. Six authored trains arrive at seconds 240, 840, 1,440, 2,040, 2,640, and 3,240, alternating platforms. Their alighters are spread across 48 seconds, with 60-second dwell. Explicit arrival events keep the chosen `magnitude` as their passenger count in every crowd assumption: a 600-person event adds 600, including under Busy. Crowd scales background demand and the six scheduled train waves, not manually entered arrival magnitudes.

The engine and import/planning validator share `src/domain/trainSchedule.ts`: same-platform trains must allow 40 seconds of approach plus 35 seconds of departure clearance. Concurrent trains on different platforms remain valid and appear together in `snapshot.trains`; the legacy single-train field retains the first active train. These are demonstrator inputs, not a KAI timetable or measured passenger volumes. The morning horizon deliberately supersedes the pack's example evening schedule.

People are anonymous agents with no names, inferred income, demographic profile, or personal targeting. A keyed hash of seed, semantic identity, and purpose produces demand, walking speed, journey, buying choice, service variability, basket, and patience. Adding a shop or changing staff does not consume or reshuffle an arrival RNG stream. Added train events have a separate identity namespace so an event named `train-1` cannot duplicate the default train's passenger IDs.

## Movement and access

The authored route graph uses hall floor `y=1`, platforms `y=0`, and the footbridge `y=2.65`. The public spine lies at `z=-2.8`, between west/east entries `x=-28/+28`. Each person receives one of seven deterministic spine lane offsets from −0.6 to +0.6 metres; rain adds the existing sheltered-side shift of up to −0.65 metres. The broad spine offset is not applied to the narrow bypass, gates, stairs, or bridge. Ticketed journeys cross the fixed gate at `(12,1,4.5)`, continue to the protected gate exit `(12,1,5.05)`, then turn toward the stair point `(8,1,6)`. Reaching that exit before turning clears the five fare pedestals with a 0.16-metre passenger body radius. The far platform uses the visible footbridge at `x=26`: near landing `(20.85,0,8.8)` → bridge `(26,2.65,8.8)` → `(26,2.65,18.5)` → far landing `(20.85,0,18.5)`. The landings are west of the first rendered stair tread; nobody walks across ground-level rails.

Shortest graph paths are cached by origin, destination, and closure state. The central edge has a valid lower concourse bypass: its long leg stays at `z=4.2` until `x=6.7`, turns to `(6.7,1,3.05)`, then reaches `(8,1,3.05)` before returning to the spine. This turn keeps the bypass below the fare pedestal bank instead of crossing the first pedestal; the central commercial bay remains reachable even with both planning courts occupied. Node arrival recalculates closure state before the next edge. A person already on an edge finishes that edge; the closure does not teleport them. The renderer and engine consume the same `shopTransform` from `src/domain/shopGeometry.ts`, using planning's single footprint table. Hall graph legs and cardinal shop entrance connections use cached obstacle visibility detours around occupied shop walls. Entry crosses the actual door line; browsing and service remain inside that rendered footprint, with transitional approaches represented as walking. Queue formation follows the local rotated storefront in at most two shallow serpentine rows, with columns based on its width, keeping even an 18-person large-shop queue clear of the public spine.

Alighters use the eight platform-facing train door positions authored in `src/data/station.ts`: `x=-21.5,-15.1,-9.3,-2.9,2.9,9.3,15.1,21.5`. Each wave cycles through those doors, with keyed horizontal variation of at most 0.15 metres. Starting centers lie on the platform side of the door thresholds at near-platform `z=10.8` and far-platform `z=16.7`. Flat platform routes use a corridor around `z=9.65` or `z=17.4`, with lane variation up to 0.18 metres. Platform connectors remain on their own platform; transferring between platforms requires the elevated bridge.

Boarders choose a deterministic door cluster and one of three waiting rows: near `z=9.5,9.82,10.14`, or far `z=17.15,17.47,17.79`. Horizontal waiting variation spans 2.8 metres around each cluster. The last cluster is shifted west, keeping its centers at or left of `x=20.85`; its boarding approach follows the clear rail-side strip beside the bridge stairs. Platform circulation also approaches the stair landings from their west ends. These positions are trajectory segments consumed by snapshots and route metrics, not decorative people or renderer-only offsets. Route checks cover occupied shop walls, platform/rail bounds, all five fare pedestals and body clearance beside bridge stairs; lane changes preserve the protected gate and stair points. Hall visibility detours use shop rectangles as obstacles. They do not perform collision checks against every fixed fixture or enforce pairwise separation between people; this is not a pedestrian collision solver.

Background journeys either cross the hall or reach a platform to board. Boarding agents wait for a matching train departure only if they have reached its platform in time. After the last available train they wait briefly and leave the modeled platform edge; the model does not assert they boarded an absent train. Every present person belongs to the model ledger. Rendering quality does not resample demand or alter those people, their trajectories, or analytical counts.

## Shops and queues

A normal journey creates only reachable, open, visible shop opportunities. Attraction depends on category configuration, unit fit, route distance, available journey dwell, and nearby same-category substitutes. Alternatives compete for one possible visit per journey. The probability of shopping is bounded at 0.58, so adding shops cannot generate unlimited buyers. Current queue load reduces acceptance at the door. Shop size limits queue space: 8, 12, or 18 waiting people.

An entered visit has a stable visit ID, entry, browsing, queue join, service start/end or abandonment, purchase decision, and exit. Queue order is FIFO. Worker count determines simultaneous service capacity. Service duration varies from 0.75–1.25 times the configured category mean. Preparation/restock task fraction increases the effective time required between served customers. Service workers already handling an order finish it when a staffing reduction begins; reduced capacity blocks new orders until active work falls below the new count. This is a non-preemptive service discipline.

Queue patience varies around category configuration. A full queue or expired patience creates a completed abandoned visit, without a transaction. Purchase probability after completed service is 0.90; a campaign does not override it. Basket values vary 0.8–1.2 around the configured basket and are rounded to integer hundreds of rupiah. Advanced tenant overrides for `basket`, `serviceSeconds`, `margin`, and `occupancyPerDay` replace their category/unit defaults.

## Interventions and campaigns

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Arrival | Add the chosen `magnitude` people at the chosen platform, without a crowd multiplier. Train dwell is bounded to 60–240 seconds; alighting is spread through the requested event duration or actual train dwell, whichever is shorter, so nobody appears after that train leaves. |
| Delay | Add `magnitude` seconds to default train arrivals whose original arrival falls in the event window; optional target `train-1`…`train-6`. |
| Rain | Intensity 0–1 slows routed movement and pulls the hall spine toward the sheltered side. |
| Route closure | Close the known central concourse edge and use its reachable bypass; canonical target `central-route`. |
| Tenant closure | A closure targeting a tenant blocks new entries and new service in that interval. |
| Staff | Signed worker change: −1 absence or +1 additional cover, targeting a tenant; capacity never falls below zero. |
| Campaign | Change a named tenant's bounded awareness/entry weight when its screen is visible along the journey and the campaign is active. |

Campaign lift is budget-dependent and saturates; spend per active hour contributes up to 0.65 lift. Objective coefficients are 1 for visits, 0.55 for awareness, and 0.8 for purchases. Purchases targets only dwell-qualified eligible entries; the purchase decision itself remains the same keyed 0.90 probability and can fail. Combined lift is capped at 0.75. Campaign spending allocates the configured budget evenly over its active window, once. Archived campaigns have no effect, cost, or tenant exposure attribution. Screen exposure counts a distinct agent–screen first route encounter. Visibility clips each actual trajectory segment against the screen's modeled radius; qualified exposure requires two visible seconds inside the report window and, for tenant attribution, the active campaign window. Neither metric claims proven attention or an audited advertising impression.

Comparison baseline is the authored opening plan with the user's seed and the same crowd assumption as Proposed, including the omitted-field legacy configuration. Proposed is an isolated copy of the selected plan. Aligned comparisons use the same report window. Stress preserves proposed changes and the crowd assumption, then attempts to add an unscaled 180-person arrival wave. It also applies one absent coffee worker at 07:20–07:30 when a coffee tenant exists, and rain intensity 0.6 during that ten-minute interval. The wave prefers 07:20–07:22:30 on the first platform, selects the other platform or nearest available slot if necessary, and respects the same train-clearance validator. A completely occupied timetable merges additional passengers into an existing arrival event up to its 1,500-person limit; a timetable with no remaining capacity retains its existing demand and applies the staffing/weather disruption. `createStressScenario` supplies exactly the world shown for the stress branch. For separately scheduled stress events, it replaces its own prior event IDs rather than stacking duplicate events. Call it from the proposed plan: the saturated-timetable fallback retains its merged passenger count, so applying it again to an already stressed output can add further passengers.

## Metrics and money

Times in configuration, event ledgers, and trajectories are **seconds**. Report `waitMean`, `waitP90`, `dwellMean`, and `workload` are **minutes**. Entry/conversion/coverage are **ratios**, not pre-multiplied percentages. Money is **integer IDR**.

Sales sum actual purchasing-event baskets in the report window. Gross margin sums each transaction's rounded basket × configured margin. Labor integrates paid scheduled worker-seconds across opening hours and staff events, then applies the category hourly wage. Rent uses an explicit 24-hour allocation: configured daily occupancy × report seconds ÷ 86,400. Campaign cost uses budget overlap. Cumulative rounding at boundaries makes cost totals additive across adjacent report windows. Contribution is exactly gross margin minus labor, occupancy, and campaign cost. It is operating contribution with these configured costs, not corporate net profit or landlord rent revenue.

Passing is distinct people with an eligible first storefront opportunity in the interval. There is at most one visit per agent. Entry rate uses entered people whose passing opportunity is also in the interval, divided by that passing cohort; this prevents arbitrary window cuts from generating rates above 100%. Completed conversion uses the completed-exit cohort, excluding unfinished visits. Transactions and sales retain purchase-event timestamps, which can precede that visit's final exit. Wait statistics include service starts in the interval and exclude abandonment; dwell uses finalized exits. Queue length is the stock at report end, equal to the matching snapshot.

Workload is actual raw service work overlapping the report plus configured required preparation/restock minutes. Coverage is paid available worker-minutes divided by required workload; it may exceed 1 when there is spare capacity. A zero workload returns 0 coverage; zero passing or completed visits returns `null` entry/conversion so the UI displays a missing denominator, never success at zero demand.

## Replay, memory, and checks

Simulation precomputes a deterministic event ledger and compact trajectory segments. Seeking reconstructs positions by binary search within each active agent's segments, without replaying thousands of frame steps. A result caches at most eight distinct normalized snapshot times. Repeating a paused time returns the same deeply frozen snapshot, including its agents, queue counts and train objects; consumer mutation cannot corrupt later reads. A new time beyond that bound evicts the oldest retained snapshot.

Metrics and 12 five-minute chart buckets use the same visit/purchase/arrival data. Analytical counts are unweighted exact model agents. The result cache retains at most six scenarios; each result caches at most 32 report queries. Captured scenarios are deeply frozen, so consumer edits cannot change cached outcomes. Validation and operator JSON preserve the optional crowd field; tenant configuration exports include that public assumption while excluding competitor tenant inputs and personal passenger records.

`npm test` includes substantive checks for non-empty demand, independent reproducibility, conservation, bounded coordinates, additive report totals, financial arithmetic, zero denominators, capacity/absence, underused staffing costs, arrival and delay timing, closure reachability, rain movement, campaign windows, archive behavior, branch isolation, unique person IDs, continuous shop movement, and wall avoidance under cardinal rotations. Crowd tests cover legacy demand preservation, Normal/Busy density, unscaled explicit arrival counts, all eight train doors, lane and waiting distribution, body clearance beside bridge stairs and all five fare pedestals, gate bypass clearance under rain with both planning courts occupied, aligned assumptions, persistence/privacy, and immutable bounded snapshot reuse. The suite first ran red against the empty engine, then added red regressions for physical route and immutability defects before their fixes.

Run `node --import tsx tools/benchmark.ts` (also `npm run benchmark` when configured) to measure cold simulation, stress, 1,000 random seeks, report queries, bounded-cache memory, and conservation. The benchmark prints the actual host and Node runtime. These are CPU/model measurements; browser software rendering cannot establish the 60 FPS target on the specified 8 GB integrated-graphics laptop. Real-device GPU profiling remains a separate delivery check.
